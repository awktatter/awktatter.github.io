Life has been getting hectic and stressful lately. And I escape into a virtual bubble of Instagram and YouTube to cope being pulled in different directions. Often times it feels like I'm running away but still tuned in, being unable to relax, and desperately craving some downtime. In those moments, I feel compelled to put some words on the paper. Nothing too fancy, creative, or profound. Just an honest reflection of the moment. To keep things light and fun, I've been exploring Haiku as a means to express myself. Here's my:

## Haiku in the oven

### First attempts
My first attempt at writing a haiku started on a random night. I was feeling restless, and sat down to meditate. But only to pen down a few lines of poetry.

> Heavy energy awash
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Still water, still breeze, still night  
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Lone candle and star

**Learnings**

Haiku is a Japanese short poetry composed of three lines with a 5-7-5 syllable structure. The topic of a haiku often captures nature in the moment. It uses concrete ideas and imagery - no metaphors, idioms, similes or emotional statements. Usually, it includes a mention of a season (kigo) and can focus on a juxtaposition of two images or ideas. More importantly, the human is an observer and not a direct participant in the poem. This form of poetry invites the reader to participate in and co-create the meaning!

My attempt isn't a haiku in the strictest sense. There is no seasonal reference and it uses abstract and emotional words. And too many syllables - 21.

> Still summer night
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; cars and dogs howl  
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Lone candle and star

An improvement over the previous one. Still some creative freedom on cars howling. But more concrete images and seasonal reference. Even mild juxtaposition.

###  A moment of waiting

A couple of weeks later, I was in the drawing room, waiting for a friend who was late. In the silence of the moment, it came to me.

> pause at chorus
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; bounded, yet unending room
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; still life

**Learnings**
Concise on the syllable count, but there is emotion and abstract ideas. Especially the "bounded, yet unending room" and "still life"! That said, this poem has a philosophical edge to it, captures an emotional state, and is human-centric. It's a senryu.

Tuning it with more concrete images from the moment (but it seems never enough!)

> Paused at chorus
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; empty room, lone chair
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; rattling windows

### Capturing moments from hometown
When I went back home, I bought a notebook to capture creative moments like this and also for sketching. Then, I found moments worth penning down as haiku/senryu. 

This one was written down to serve as a title for Haiku entries in my book. It's more of a senryu than a haiku because it uses figurative language about how haikus are made and it's human-centric with the self-deprecating humor. 

> Haiku in the oven
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; toasty and charred
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; smoke everywhere

As I was cooking this up, I was also scrambling around for a pen to write in my newly christened notebook. Some more "poetic" words capturing the moment.

> Many pens on the table
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; but none to ink the paper with words
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; enter the lone pink pen

Followed by

> Words inked in pink
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; With the blue pen besides
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; questionable choice made

**Learnings**
Too wordy and uses a play on playwriting. I also use figurative language with "ink the paper with words". Even if I mostly talk about using concrete ideas (the pens, colors), there is a sense of a human (me) in the picture. This is really evident in "questionable choice made". I have fixed the perspective and interpretation for my reader. All of these point to a senryu rather than a haiku.

And a final one for that day, and one that makes for a solid haiku

> Humid summer night
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; One cat in hiding
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; another arrives
